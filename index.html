Technology — definition
Collection of techniques, skills, methods, and processes used in production of goods/services or accomplishment of objectives.
Simplest form = development and use of basic tools
Application of science & engineering to enhance human conditions
Management — definition
Process of Planning, Organizing, Coordinating, Leading, and Controlling
Setting strategy + coordinating employee efforts to accomplish objectives
Uses financial, natural, technological, and human resources
Technology Management — definition Important
A set of management disciplines that allows organizations to manage their technological fundamentals to create competitive advantage. (NRC, 1987)
Includes: planning, directing, control, coordination of technology development
Has Hard aspects (science/engineering) and Soft aspects (processes)
Aims to maximize cost-effectiveness of technology investments
Science vs Technology
Science = study of WHY natural things happen
Technology = USE of knowledge to turn resources into goods/services
Technology word comes from Greek: techne (art) + logos (logic/science)
Why study Technology?
Technology satisfies basic human needs and wants
Responsible for great deal of human progress
Key tool to gain competitive advantage in global economy
Application of technology (not just development) = key to success
Challenges of TM
Increasing cost
Complexity
Pace of technology advancement
Diversity of technology sources
Globalized competition
Impact of Information Technology
TM Framework — 6 Core Activities Important
Remember: A E I L P S — Acquisition, Exploitation, Identification, Learning, Protection, Selection
These 6 activities are linked to 3 core business processes: Strategy, Innovation, and Operation
1. Acquisition
How a company obtains the technologies it needs for business
Internal Development (R&D) — creating technology in-house
Co-Development — joint development with partner
External Acquisition — purchasing/licensing from outside

R&D Goals

Generate new knowledge and technical ideas (research)
Transform ideas into working products/services (development)

External Acquisition Options

Purchasing, Subcontracting, Licensing, Alliances, M&A (Merger & Acquisition)
2. Exploitation
Achieving profit or benefits from technology
Commercialization — transferring invention to sellable product
Technology Transfer — moving technology from creator to applier
Technology Utilization — maintaining, adjusting, improving technologies
3. Identification
Spotting technologies and their applications to the business
Technology planning, forecasting, foresight, road mapping, intelligence
Includes market changes + technological developments

Classification of Technology

Enabling → Key → Pacing → Emerging

Technology Roadmapping — 2 forms

Planning: How to reach a goal? (market focus, customer driven)
Forecasting: What opportunities could arise? (technology focus, open ended)

Patent Analysis
A patent = rights granted by Government to inventor for a fixed period (generally 20 years, renewed every 5 years)
Patent Search = access patentability, novelty, infringement, validity
Patent Analysis = convert statistical patent info into useful strategic info

4 Steps of Patent Analysis

1. Patent search → 2. Categorization → 3. Visualization → 4. Integration into strategic decisions

Used for TM activities

Acquisition, Identification, Protection
Portfolio Management
Centralized management of projects/programs to achieve strategic business goals
Portfolio = bundle of projects/programs grouped together
Project = definable objective under 3 constraints: Time, Cost, Quality

3 Steps

1. Individual project analysis → 2. Optimal portfolio selection → 3. Portfolio adjustments

Used for TM activities

Acquisition, Learning, Selection
Road Mapping
An extended look at the future of a field using collective knowledge of experts — shown as a graphical chart
Provides high-level summary of strategic elements (one page overview)
Major benefit = communication during development and dissemination
Addresses: Identification, Exploitation, Learning
S-Curve
Growth curve — analogy with biological life cycle of a technology

3 Steps

1. Collect longitudinal data on performance metric
2. Observe evolution and plot the curve
3. Use curve for decision making
Used for: Acquisition, Exploitation, Identification
TM vs Innovation Management Important
Innovation = doing something new (product, process, service) within a firm
Innovation is NOT limited to technology — can be organizational
Open Innovation = companies can buy/license from other companies too
TM and Innovation overlap ONLY when there is a technology innovation
Otherwise they have separate disciplinary body of knowledge
4 Types of Innovation (Oslo Manual 1995) Important
Product Innovation — new/improved good or service (specs, materials, components)
Process Innovation — new/improved production or delivery method (technique, equipment, software)
Marketing Innovation — new marketing method (design, packaging, pricing, placement)
Organizational Innovation — new organizational method to reduce costs, improve satisfaction
3 Newer Innovation Types
Introduced in recent years, relevant to technology managers
Eco-Innovation — products/processes contributing to sustainable development with minimal resources and toxic releases
Reverse Innovation — innovate in developing markets (China, India) then distribute globally
Design-Driven Innovation — based on user experience and meaning; creates new markets. Example: Apple iPod
Innovation Management
Successful implementation of novel ideas forming different innovation types within an organization
Comprises all steps: development, introduction, implementation of new products/processes
Allows organizations to respond to opportunities using creativity
Includes planning, decision-making, organizational and control tasks
Example: Amazon book delivery over Internet (Service Innovation)
4. Learning Important
Ability to learn and continuously improve technological capability — crucial to TM
Occurs at every level in an organization
Learning = a dynamic capability
= acquisition/use of existing knowledge + creation of new knowledge to improve economic performance
5 Types of Learning
Learn by Doing — manufacturing activities, skill acquisition, learning curve
Learn by Searching — commercial dimension; searching product characteristics required by market
Scientific Learning — basic research, acquiring fundamental laws of science
Learn by Using — feedback from customers on new product performance
Spillover Learning — knowledge from journals, experienced personnel, university research
Knowledge Important
Knowledge = know-what (recognition) + know-how (capacity to act) + know-why (understanding)
Tacit Knowledge
Difficult to transfer
Hidden, unspoken
From personal experience
Example: making a cookie
Explicit Knowledge
Tangible
Can be documented
Diagrams, books, charts
Tacit ↔ Explicit Interactions (SECI) Important
Socialization — sharing tacit knowledge between individuals (tacit → tacit)
Externalization — converting tacit to explicit (tacit → explicit)
Combination — converting explicit to more complex explicit (explicit → explicit)
Internalization — converting explicit to tacit (explicit → tacit)
5. Protection
Protecting knowledge and expertise embedded in products, services, and manufacturing systems
Legally protected assets — e.g. Patents
Intangible assets — e.g. Know-how

Intangible Assets — 3 categories

Competencies (skill, know-how, leadership)
Innovation and entrepreneurship
Ability to adapt
Intellectual Property (IP)
IP laws vary from country to country
Harmonized through international treaties
Key treaty: WTO Agreement, 1994
Technology Transfer (TT)
Process by which technology, knowledge, and information developed by a creator is applied and utilized by an applier. (Khalil, 2000)

5 Types

Company to Company
Non-profit to Company
R&D to Manufacturing (within same org)
Individual to Department
International Technology Transfer

Issues in TT

Integration, Utilization, Organizational issues, Deal & Negotiations, Commercialization, Regulations, Learning (absorptive capacity)
6. Selection
Selecting technologies that help organizations achieve their ultimate goals
Important process of TM
Involves evaluating and choosing from available technology options
TM Key Concepts (Typical)
Technology Strategy — logic/role of technology in organization
Technology Forecasting — identifying possible relevant technologies
Technology Roadmap — mapping technologies to business/market needs
Technology Project Portfolio — set of projects under development
Technology Portfolio — set of technologies currently in use
